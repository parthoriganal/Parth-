## 6. Paperback economics and the zero-marketing constraint

KDP states that standard paperback royalties on Amazon are `(royalty rate × list price) − printing cost`; for Amazon.com, the standard rate is 50% at list prices at or below $9.98 and 60% at or above $9.99. Printing cost varies by trim, ink, page count, and marketplace. [KDP paperback royalty rules](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834330) · [KDP printing-cost table](https://kdp.amazon.com/en_US/help/topic/G201834340).

- **Knife title illustration:** at the public $7.99 / 120-page listing, an assumed regular-trim black-ink print cost gives about **$1.56** royalty per standard Amazon sale; this is an estimate under assumed print settings, not the private dashboard amount.
- **Hypothetical 180-page standard-trim B&W paperback at $14.99:** estimated printing cost = $1 + (180 × $0.012) = **$3.16**; estimated Amazon royalty = ($14.99 × 0.60) − $3.16 = **$5.83**. This is arithmetic only, not a chosen price or sales forecast.
- KDP print-on-demand removes the need for inventory purchase, but not the costs of the author's time, editing, cover, research, software, or the opportunity cost of low/no sales.
- With no promotion or audience, the success variable is whether Amazon supplies relevant impressions and converts them. A plausible margin per copy does not establish impressions, conversion, or units. No monthly sales estimate can responsibly be made here.

## 7. Hostile audit

The strongest skeptical explanation for the three reported sales is not “these niches work”; it is “three isolated orders occurred, and their sources, repeatability, and net royalties are unknown.” The knife title’s placement at the top of one query could reflect personalization, recency, exact wording, or the small observed result set. The menopause ebook's broad search-trend context is not evidence of paperback buying. Both products are recent, so the available observation window is short.

For any new topic, the hostile case is similar: a task can be evergreen while people use free apps, community spreadsheets, manufacturer manuals, official guidance, or a well-known specialist author instead of a paperback. Amazon can display a book without giving it enough impressions to sell; books with rank snapshots do not tell us if sales are organic or sustained. A notebook may be easy to create but hard to distinguish and can fail to deliver enough perceived value. Seasonal activity can look like a trend in a short window. If buyer review evidence and historical search series are absent, a proposed “gap” is only author intuition.

**Hostile-audit conclusion:** the evidence does not support choosing one new niche under the user's strict no-marketing condition. It also does not support converting the knife/menopause sales into a repeatable business model.
