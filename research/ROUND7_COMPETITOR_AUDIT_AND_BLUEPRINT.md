Consensus across all four briefed competitors + both Peter Pauper titles: personal details, key

## 2.4 Verdict on competition

**This is not a market to enter with a "better designed" version of the same book.** It is a market
with a 15,000-review, $9.95, archival, tabbed hardcover that ships Prime and lies flat. The entry
has to be **structurally different in a way a paper product genuinely cannot copy** — and paper
products can copy page layouts almost instantly.

# 4. Final book concept

## 4.1 The positioning decision

**Do not compete on completeness.** Six products already cover the same 17 sections and one of them
has 15,120 reviews. Completeness is a saturated attribute.

**Compete on what happens next.** The single defensible structural claim available in this market:

> Every book in this category is a **container**. *The Family Records Organizer* is a
> **container plus a procedure** — it tells the person who opens it, in order, exactly what to
> do with what is inside.

**Working title:** *The Family Records Organizer*
**Subtitle (working):** *Everything your family needs to know, and exactly what to do with it*

## 4.2 The four structural differentiators

Each is a thing **no competitor's published contents list contains** (VERIFIED by absence), and each
is cheap to produce:

1. **The First 48 Hours** — a single, laminated-feel, one-page action sheet at the very front,
   organised for the *reader*, not the owner: who to call in what order, which document unlocks
   which next step, and what can safely wait a week. Peter Pauper's "At the Time of My Passing" is
   the only partial attempt and sits among 17 tabs.
2. **Document-first, not account-first organisation.** The book is built around a **Document
   Locator** — a master table of the ~20 documents a family actually needs — and every other
   section cross-references it, so "where is it" is always answerable in one lookup.
3. **Digital assets as assets, with a hard safety rule.** Account → custodian → what happens on
   death → **whether it has a legacy/nominee feature** → *where the credential is held*, never the
   credential. This replaces the password fields that three competitors publish.
4. **A closed loop: locator + review date + "someone else knows this exists."** A tear-out card
   naming who holds the book and where; a dated annual review page. Every competitor ships a book
   that can be lost with nobody knowing it existed.

## 4.3 What it deliberately does **not** do

- No legal advice. No state-specific will/trust/POD instructions beyond *what to ask a professional*.
- No filled-in "your state requires X" claims.
- No password fields. Ever.
- No medical advice.
- No forms that duplicate documents the reader already has (no blank will, no blank POA).
- No four-colour interior — see §7.
- No "funny"/novelty tone (that is a distinct, already-occupied segment: B0F7HM7R2Q, #1 Elder
  Family Law, 332 reviews).

## 4.5 Why 128 pages, and why not 110

The brief says ~110–140. Within that, **128** is the mathematically clean choice:

- It is a multiple of **16**, so it prints as exactly **8 signatures** with no blank filler pages.
- It lands at the extent of the market leader (96) **plus** the space needed for the procedure
  pages — the differentiator — without becoming the "too overwhelming" failure mode that Ava
  Brinley's listing explicitly positioned against ("just the right length to not be overwhelming").
- It reads as substantial next to 65- and 32-page competitors without competing with a 96-page
  hardcover on page count alone.

Royalty at 128 pp (verified against `backend/kdp_engine/profit.py`, KDP-constructed constants
`fixed_bw 0.85 + pages × 0.012`):

| Price | Printing | **Royalty** |
|---|---|---|
| $10.99 | $2.39 | **$4.21** |
| **$11.99** | $2.39 | **$4.81** |
| $12.99 | $2.39 | $5.41 |

At **$11.99** you net **$4.81**. The 65-page, $9.99 Wairimu book nets
$9.99 × 0.60 − $1.63 = **$4.36** (65 pp printing = 0.85 + 65 × 0.012 = $1.63).

So the 128-page book at $11.99 nets **$0.45 more per unit (+10.3%)** *and* carries roughly twice the
perceived substance. That is the whole economic argument for the extent, and it is arithmetic, not
opinion. Note what this means honestly: **the extra 63 pages are nearly free to the author and
almost entirely free to the reader** — the margin gain is small, and the real gain is perceived
value, not revenue. Do not overstate it.

# 6. Section-by-section blueprint: worksheet structures and reader workflow

## 6.1 The reader workflow (the thing to get right)

The owner does not read this book front to back, and the family member does not read it at all.
Design for three distinct entry points:

```
OWNER, DAY 1        →   pp. 2–5    How to use + First 48 Hours + "tell two people"
OWNER, WORKING      →   Part 2 first (assets), then Part 1 (documents), then Part 3 (wishes)
                        because assets are what people can actually remember from memory,
                        and documents are what they have to go and find.
FAMILY, DAY 1       →   p. 4      THE FIRST 48 HOURS. Nothing else.
FAMILY, DAY 30      →   pp. 87–107 Part 4, in order
```

**Rule: the book must be usable by someone who has read only one page.** The First 48 Hours sheet
therefore carries its own mini-index and a "turn to page X for Y" footer on every line.

## 6.2 Worksheet design system (apply to all 34 sections)

Every record page follows the same six-part template, so the reader learns it once:

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| 1. Section title + one-line *why this matters* | 18 pt bold / 11 pt italic |
| 2. **"You will need" strip** | What to have in front of you (e.g. *a recent statement*) — 9 pt |
| 3. Record grid | Ruled rows, 24–28 pt row height, **large print**, no grey screens |
| 4. **Cross-reference field** | Always ends with *"Document Locator row #___"* |
| 5. **"Check with a professional if…"** flag | Fixed icon + one line; never advice |
| 6. Footer | *Section · page · last updated ___* |

**Typography for a 55+ reader (this is a purchase driver, not a nicety):**
body **11.5–12 pt**, row labels **12 pt**, headings **18–24 pt**, effective leading 14–15 pt,
minimum 0.5 in inner margin, **no line under 8.5 in-wide that requires the eye to track more than
7 in**.

## 6.3 Four exemplar page specs

**(a) The Document Locator — pp. 8–11**
Landscape-on-portrait wide table, 20 fixed rows over 4 pages (5 rows/page at comfortable size).
Columns: `Document` (pre-printed, so the reader never has to think of it) · `Have it?` ▢ · `Where
exactly` (2 lines) · `Original / copy` ▢▢ · `Who else knows` (1 line) · `If missing, ask` (1 line).
Pre-printing all 20 document names is the single highest-value design decision in the book: it
removes the blank-page problem that makes these products get abandoned half-filled.

**(b) Digital assets — p. 51**
One row per service, 5 rows/page:
`Service ____ | What it's for ____ | Account name ____ | Legacy/nominee setting? Y/N | Credential held where ____ | On death: memorialise / download / close | Who else has access ____`
With a bordered, high-contrast box at top: **"Do not write a password, PIN, or seed phrase in this
book."**

**(c) First 48 Hours — p. 4**
Numbered 1–8, each with a right-hand "needs first" column:
1. Call ___ (the named first contact). 2. Find the will — Document Locator row ___.
3. Get 10 original death certificates (a plain *how*, with the free .gov pointer).
4. Secure the home, pets, vehicle. 5. Notify employer and bank (why: auto-deposits continue).
6. Do not pay or promise anything yet. 7. Do not distribute property. 8. Nothing else is urgent.
Each line ends with `→ page ___`.

**(d) Personal property — pp. 74–77**
`Item | Where it is | Who I want to have it | Already promised? | In my will?`
Plus a fixed banner: *"This page records my wishes. It does not change my will."*

# 7. KDP production considerations

## 7.1 Trim, bleed, and margin spec (verified arithmetic)

- Trim **8.5 × 11 in**; no bleed on interior (KDP requires bleed only for edge-to-edge elements —
  **do not use full-bleed interior art**, it raises cost and risk with no benefit here).
- **Gutter (inside margin):** 128 pp is in KDP's 151–300 tier boundary? — No: 128 pp falls in the
  **24–150 page** tier, which requires a minimum **0.375 in inside margin** for no-bleed. Spec
  **0.75 in** inside for a workbook that must lie open and be written in, **0.5 in** outside,
  **0.6 in** top/bottom. *The 0.375 in minimum is wrong for this product; writing in the gutter is
  the #1 usability failure of cheap workbooks.*
- **Spine width at 128 pp, white paper:** 128 × 0.002252 = **0.288 in**.
- **Full cover wrap:** (2 × 8.5) + 0.288 + 0.25 bleed = **17.538 × 11.25 in**.
  *(Cream paper would use 0.0025 in/page → 0.32 in spine; white is specified because it makes
  large-type ruled rows read cleaner.)*

## 7.2 Colour, paper, ink

**Black & white interior, mandatory.** A 4-colour 8.5×11 at 128 pp costs $0.85 + 128×$0.025 =
**$4.05** to print, which at $11.99 leaves **$3.14** — a 35% royalty cut versus $4.81 B&W. There is
no colour-dependent content in this design. White paper over cream: ruled rows and grey-scale
icons reproduce more crisply, and white is the cheaper default.

## 7.3 File and font production

- **Output:** PDF/X-1a:2001 or PDF/X-4, embedded fonts, 300 dpi for the ~20 line icons.
- **Toolchain already present and licence-checked in the repo's earlier audit:** WeasyPrint v70.0
  (BSD-3) for HTML→PDF with precise `@page` margin control, reportlab v5.1.0 (BSD) for the ruled
  grids, pypdf for imposition/verification. All free, all offline. **LanguageTool (LGPL-2.1) is the
  one dependency with obligations — if used, keep it as an external tool, do not vendor its code.**
- **Fonts:** must be OFL or otherwise licensed for embedding. Do **not** use a system font you have
  not licence-checked. Two families maximum.
- **Grids must be drawn as rules, not typed underscores or tables of dots.** Underscores reflow and
  break; this is the craft failure visible in cheap competitors.

## 7.4 The ISBN / imprint decision — flagged, not resolved

Two live options, and this is a business decision that is **yours**:

| Option | Cost | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **KDP free ISBN** | $0 | Imprint shows as "Independently published". Cannot be used with another printer later. |
| **Own ISBN** | **Paid** | Not permitted under the $0 rule. |

**Recommendation under the constraint: use the free KDP ISBN**, accept "Independently published",
and note that this is precisely what Wairimu did and it did not stop 930 reviews. **This is the
only point in the plan where the $0 rule forces a visible quality compromise.**

## 7.5 The category decision — the highest-leverage item in this document

**Verified:** the two largest competitors (15,120 and 9,743 reviews) are filed in
**Office Products → Planners**, not in Books. The four indie competitors are in Books.

Therefore:

- **Primary category: Books → Self-Help → Death, Grief & Loss.** This is where the searchable
  buyers are, and it is where the four briefed competitors rank.
- **Add Office Products as a second category if KDP permits the dual placement — this needs
  verification against current KDP category rules before publishing.** If dual placement is not
  permitted, choose **Books**, because the keyword-targeted buyers you can reach organically are
  there.
- **Do not file under Law.** Wairimu files under Right to Die / Estate Planning Laws and it has not
  helped him beat a stationery planner; it also invites legal-claim scrutiny the book cannot carry.

## 7.6 Keywords and listing metadata (working, to be tested not asserted)

Buyer phrases in evidence: *final wishes planner · end of life planner · estate planning organizer ·
when I die · information my family needs to know · life binder · after death planner · death
organizer · important documents organizer · what my family should know*.

- **Title:** keep *The Family Records Organizer* and put buyer language in the subtitle.
- **Do not** use "I'm Dead, Now What" or "Everything You Need to Know When I'm Gone" — both are
  live titles of large competitors.
- **A+ content** is free and the leaders use it heavily; it is a table-stakes production task, not
  an optional extra.
- **Do not run ads.** Per your standing constraints.

## 7.7 Print-probe and proofing (free)

- **Use KDP's free previewer** on the uploaded PDF before ordering anything.
- **Physical proof:** KDP offers author copies **at printing cost**, which for 128 pp B&W is about
  **$2.39**. That is not $0, so **do not order one without your approval** — but the gutter and
  large-type decisions above are exactly the ones that cannot be validated on screen.

# 8. Sections requiring factual verification, and how to verify each free

**This is the most important table in the document.** Nothing in the book may be published as fact
until its row is checked.

| # | Section | The factual risk | Free verification route |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First 48 Hours — "get N death certificates" | Practices and recommended counts vary by state and by institution | State vital-records .gov sites (all 50 publish fees and processes); SSA.gov for the SSA notification step. **Phrase as "many institutions ask for an original — check with each," never a fixed number.** |
| 2 | Part 4 — probate vs non-probate, "what beneficiaries can expect" | **Highest legal risk in the book.** Varies enormously by state. | Do **not** state rules. | 3 | Cancellation worksheet — credit bureaus, SSA, DMV, passport, voter roll | Addresses and procedures change | **AnnualCreditReport.com**, **SSA.gov**, state DMV sites, **travel.state.gov** (passport), **eac.gov** (voter). Verify each URL live on the day of writing. |

| 5 | Glossary (24 terms) | Definitions drift; "probate" and "trustee" are state-loaded | **Cornell LII Wex** (free, citable), **consumerfinance.gov**, **irs.gov** for tax terms. One line each, no state variation claimed. |

| 8 | Medical section | Must not imply medical advice | Restrict to *recording* provider names, medications, allergies, and where the directive lives. No dosing, no guidance. |
| 9 | Obituary worksheet | Newspaper/platform conventions vary | Keep it as slots only; no format claim. |

pointer to an official free source, or (c) deleted. There is no fourth category.

## The one thing I would not proceed without

**R1.** Every other risk has a design answer. R1 is physical: a perfect-bound 8.5 × 11 paperback
