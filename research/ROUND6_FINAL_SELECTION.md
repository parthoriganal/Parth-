## 1. Final selected niche

# End-of-life & final-wishes organizer ("estate organizer / life binder for your family")

**Product concept:** an 8.5 × 11 paperback, **~110–140 pages**, priced **$9.99–$11.99**, positioned as
*the complete record your family needs when you're gone* — the modern successor to the 2020–2022
generation of planners.

**Buyer-intent keywords (measured):** `my final wishes planner` (6,000+ results) ·
`estate planning organizer` (9,000+ results) · `final wishes planner` · `end of life planner` ·
`estate planning workbook` · `when i die` (autocompletes to a brand name — see risks) ·
`information my family needs to know` · `after death planner` · `life binder` → `end of life binder`

## 2. Why it is the strongest choice

1. **It has the deepest sales evidence of anything measured in six rounds.** 930 / 1,100 / 1,229 /
   163 reviews across four independent publishers, at $7.98–$10.99. No other candidate in this project
   has more than one title above 200 reviews.
2. **The best-performing entry is a 65-page self-published paperback at $9.99.** Page count is not the
   barrier to sales here; structure is. That is the single most encouraging fact for a $0 producer.
3. **Evergreen is as strong as demand gets** — it is driven by mortality, not by fashion, season or
   technology.
4. **Breadth of confirmed buyer language:** at least eight distinct phrasings, and a *brand name* has
   become an autocomplete term — the strongest possible signal that people search this category by
   intent rather than browsing into it.
5. **The incumbents are beatable on quality, not on marketing.** The 930-review leader sits at
   **4.3** and the 1,100-review title at 4.5 — good but not excellent, in a category where the
   purchase is emotionally weighty and completeness matters enormously.

**Assessment: Supported (strongly, by inference).**

## 5. Main uncertainties and risks

**Uncertainties**
1. **No absolute volume figure exists** for free. My Requirement-3 case rests on review depth, result counts and brand-level autocomplete — strong, but not a number.
2. **Organic vs advertising is not cleanly separable.** Advertisers are present. I argue the *review leaders* are non-advertisers, but I cannot prove it.
3. **Review-to-sales ratio is unknown** for low-content books, so I make no sales estimate.
4. **Recency of velocity unknown** — I have cumulative reviews, not last-6-month movement.

**Risks**
1. **Clever Fox is an entrenched brand** — it *is* the autocomplete term for `when i die`. Beating a brand on its own keyword is hard without ads.
2. **Price compression** — a $4.00 competitor (B0BQ9JB2LM) exists and the shelf runs $7.98–$10.99. Margin is thin per unit; this is a volume business.
3. **Sensitivity of the theme** — some buyers find it morbid; it also sells strongly as a gift, which cuts both ways.
4. **Legal care required** — estate law is state-specific. The book must be framed as an *organizer and record-keeper*, not legal advice, with explicit "consult an attorney" guidance. This is a real content risk, not a formality.
5. **Differentiation must be earned.** The incumbents are 4.3–4.6, not 4.9 — they are beatable, but only by genuinely better structure.
6. **Ads may be needed early** to get initial velocity, which conflicts with a pure-organic plan.

## 6. Best paperback concept for this niche

**Working concept:** *"The Family Records Organizer — Everything They'll Need to Know, In One Place"*
110–140 pp · 8.5 × 11 · paperback · **$10.99–11.99**

**The differentiation is a real, defensible gap.** The review leaders were published in **2020 and
2022**, before digital-asset inheritance became a mainstream problem. A 2026 organizer should cover
what they largely miss:

| Section | Why it wins |
|---|---|
| **"First 48 Hours"** — a one-page action sheet for whoever finds this book | No incumbent opens with the reader's actual moment of need; it is the strongest structural differentiator available |
| **Digital asset inventory** — accounts, subscriptions, cloud storage, crypto, password-manager handover, 2FA recovery | The 2020/2022 leaders predate this; genuinely under-served |
| **Document locator** — where the will, deed, insurance, and POA physically are | Turns the book into a map, not a container |
| **People & professionals** — attorney, CPA, financial advisor, executor, funeral home | Practical, and drives repeat use |
| **Financial & obligations register** — accounts, debts, recurring payments, benefits | Prevents the family's most common post-death problem |
| **Care & medical wishes** — advance directives, medication, physicians | Highest-emotion section; handles it with dignity |
| **Funeral, obituary and personal messages** | The section buyers actually buy the book for |
| **State-document checklist** — what to gather, with "verify with an attorney" framing | Adds real value without giving legal advice |
| **Letters / legacy pages** | Turns a form into a keepsake — the gift rationale |

**Format decisions that matter:** lay-flat-friendly page design, generous writing space, an index,
and **large, legible type** (the buyer is often 55+ or grieving). Every page should be usable, which
is precisely where a 65-page book rated 4.3 leaves room.
