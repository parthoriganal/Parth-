## Rule 2 — Evergreen: **strongest evidence of any niche I examined**

- Demand is driven by **demographics**, not fashion: the US 65+ population is the fastest-growing cohort, and family caregiving is a normal life event rather than a trend.
- **No seasonality, no news dependence, no technology substitution.** A binder does not get replaced by an app the way a mileage logbook or password book does — caregivers overwhelmingly still manage paper records and hand off verbally.

**Confidence: high.** This is the one rule free data can genuinely settle.

## Risks — the case against

1. **Crowded and commoditised angle.** In round-3 research I found **≥7 listings published May–Oct 2026** already marketing handoff/sibling coordination. The obvious wedge is taken; differentiation must come from scope and completeness, not concept.
2. **Price compression at the bottom.** A **$4.99** competitor (B0DV9TS3G8, 4.3/80) sits in the same shelf, and plain logs sell at $8.99. At $13.99 you must justify the premium with genuine structure.
3. **Thin royalty per unit.** A $13.99 200-page 8.5×11 paperback yields roughly **$3–4** after print cost (estimate — depends on final page count and KDP print pricing; I have not verified your exact configuration). This is a volume business, not a margin business.
4. **Free printables and hospital paperwork** are real substitutes. The binder must win on portability, durability and gift-worthiness — it is often bought by an adult child in a crisis moment.
5. **Buyer is stressed, not browsing.** Caregivers convert on clarity and speed of use, not on features. A confusing binder fails despite good demand.
6. **Ads likely needed early.** Organic rank for a new title in a 20,000-title category takes time; the incumbents are buying ads.
7. **Rule 3 absolute volume remains unmeasured.** My confidence rests on proxies, not on a number.
