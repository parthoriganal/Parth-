## 1. THE FIVE NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

These govern every technique in Chapters 7–11. If a drafted sentence conflicts with one of the five, the sentence is wrong.

| # | Rule | Why | Where it is enforced |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **No self-manipulation of the cervical spine.** No thrust, no end-range rotation, no "cracking," no forcing. | Meyer 2026: 24 cases, 2 deaths, half precipitated by neck pain | Ch 9 "Never do this" panel; Ch 6 list |
| **2** | **No pressure on the front or sides of the neck, the airway, the carotid, or the bony spine.** | Vascular and airway structures are superficial; no therapeutic benefit justifies contact | Ch 9 contact map; Ch 11 spine rule |
| **3** | **No pushing through pain.** Working window is 3–4/10. Sharp, radiating, or increasing pain is a stop signal. | Pain modulates pain and increases guarding; benefit does not scale with intensity | Ch 8 scale; every "Stop and check" box |
| **4** | **Any neurological symptom stops the session and triggers assessment.** Numbness, tingling, weakness, or pain travelling down an arm or leg. | Nerve tissue is not a self-treatment target | Ch 8, 9, 10, 11 |
| **5** | **When in doubt, don't.** | The reader cannot examine themselves; the asymmetry of risk favours stopping | Ch 6 opening; Ch 15 closing |

## 2. RED FLAGS — SCREENING CONTENT FOR CHAPTER 6

### 2.1 🔴 Emergency — same day / emergency department

Presented in the book as a short, scannable block. **Not softened. Not buried.**

- **Saddle or perineal numbness** (numbness between the legs, around the genitals or anus)
- **New bladder or bowel dysfunction** — difficulty passing urine, retention, incontinence, or loss of bowel control
- **Bilateral leg weakness or numbness**
- **Sudden severe neck or head pain** — particularly with neck movement, or a "worst ever" headache
- **New dizziness, double vision, slurred speech, facial numbness, or difficulty swallowing** — especially after neck movement or neck trauma
- **Chest pain, breathlessness, or pain with exertion**
- **Unexplained fever with severe back pain**
- **New leg weakness or foot drop**
- **Calf that is swollen, hot, red, and tender** — possible deep vein thrombosis

**Source basis:** cauda equina consensus — perineal sensory change and bladder/bowel dysfunction appear in **all** included guidelines; radicular pain in 8/9; motor weakness in 5/9; urgent MRI on suspicion (claim 30). Vascular warning signs: claims 26–28.

**Non-negotiable framing (Ch 6):** after this list, the book states that serious spinal pathology occurs in **under 1%** of primary care presentations (claim 31). The list exists to avoid missing something rare, **not** because something rare is likely. *Both sentences must appear together. The list without the counterweight induces fear; the counterweight without the list is negligent.*

### 2.2 🟠 See a clinician soon — days, not weeks

- Pain that has worsened steadily over 2–3 weeks despite sensible activity
- Night pain that consistently wakes you
- Unexplained weight loss
- Pain following a significant fall, collision, or accident
- A history of cancer with new back or bone pain
- Progressive stiffness with systemic symptoms
- Any symptom that frightens you

### 2.3 🟡 Modify or avoid — conditional on the reader

| Condition | Modification |
|---|---|
| **Pregnancy** | Avoid supine work after the first trimester; avoid abdominal pressure; consult before starting |
| **Anticoagulants / antiplatelets / bleeding disorder** | Reduce pressure substantially; bruising risk; clinician advice first |
| **Reduced sensation (peripheral neuropathy, spinal cord injury, diabetes with neuropathy)** | **Do not use pressure you cannot feel accurately** — the feedback loop that makes this safe is absent |
| **Severe osteoporosis** | Avoid loading over bone; avoid spinal pressure entirely |
| **Recent surgery** | No work on or near the site until cleared |
| **Known/suspected DVT** | **Absolute exclusion in the affected limb** (97% expert agreement, claim 29) |
| **Peripheral arterial disease** | Avoid calf pressure |
| **Varicose veins** | Avoid direct pressure |
| **Active cancer** | Clinician advice before any technique |
| **Hypermobility spectrum / hEDS** | Reduce end-range work; prioritise control over range |
| **Acute injury, bruise, swelling, open wound, skin infection** | Avoid the area entirely (73–97% expert agreement, claim 29) |
| **Fever or acute illness** | Defer |

### 2.4 ⚪ Absolute local contraindications (from expert consensus)
Local tissue inflammation **97%** · deep vein thrombosis **97%** · bone infection **94%** · myositis ossificans **92%** · bone fracture **84%** · open wounds **73%** (claim 29).

## 3. PER-REGION CONTACT RULES

### 3.1 Neck — the highest-risk region
**Permitted:** lateral and posterior *shoulder* tissue; upper trapezius **at the shoulder, not the neck**; upper thoracic paraspinals; thoracic mobility with a towel.
**Prohibited:** anterior triangle (carotid, airway); posterior cervical spine directly; suboccipital region with pressure; any end-range rotation; any thrust; any technique that reproduces dizziness, visual change, or nausea.
**Special rule:** the book teaches **working around the neck, not on it.** Stated explicitly in Ch 9 as a deliberate limitation.

### 3.2 Shoulder
**Permitted:** posterior deltoid, infraspinatus region, upper trapezius, rhomboids, thoracic paraspinals.
**Prohibited:** axilla (brachial plexus, axillary vessels); anterior shoulder over the biceps tendon sheath in acute pain; any point producing arm tingling.

### 3.3 Lower back
**Permitted:** paraspinal tissue lateral to the spine, ball **against a wall**, controlled load.
**Prohibited:** direct pressure on spinous processes; full body weight on a ball on the floor; any work during acute severe pain.
**Rationale for the book:** the thoracolumbar fascia is thick and largely not accessible to a ball, which makes the caution easy to justify honestly.

### 3.4 Hip and gluteal
**Permitted:** gluteus maximus and medius, lateral thigh (vastus lateralis), seated or wall-supported.
**Prohibited:** sustained deep pressure in the deep gluteal region (sciatic nerve path); direct heavy pressure on the iliotibial band (painful, mechanically pointless); the fibular head (common peroneal nerve); the femoral triangle.
**Stop signal:** any reproduction of leg pain, tingling, or numbness.

### 3.5 Calf
**Permitted:** gastrocnemius, **seated, gentle**, only if no DVT risk factors.
**Prohibited:** any calf pressure with risk factors present; any calf with unilateral swelling, heat, redness, or tenderness (emergency assessment).

### 3.6 Foot
**Permitted:** plantar surface, seated, ball under the arch and midfoot.
**Prohibited:** direct pressure on a bony prominence with acute pain; the dorsum of the foot.

## 4. STOP-SIGNAL LIST (used verbatim in every "Stop and check" box)

Stop immediately if you notice:
1. Sharp or stabbing pain
2. Pain travelling down an arm or a leg
3. Numbness, tingling, or pins and needles
4. Weakness or loss of control
5. Dizziness, visual change, nausea, or a headache that builds
6. A hot, burning, or electric sensation
7. Any of the emergency signs in Chapter 6

And the next-day rule: **soreness lasting a few hours is common. A flare that lasts into the next day means less pressure and less time — not more.**

## 6. LANGUAGE RULES FOR SAFETY TEXT

**Required:**
- Plain, short sentences. One instruction per sentence.
- "Stop" used as an imperative, not a suggestion.
- Numbers with units.
- Uncertainty stated as uncertainty ("the incidence is unknown," "no study has measured this").

**Prohibited:**
- Conditional softening of a red flag ("if you happen to notice...")
- Burying a warning mid-paragraph
- "Consult a professional" as the *only* safety content
- Any reassurance that contradicts a red flag
- Implying a technique is safe *because* it is gentle
- Describing a symptom and then explaining why it is probably fine

## 9. WHAT THIS PLAN DOES NOT DO

- It does not diagnose anything.
- It does not replace individual assessment.
- It does not certify any technique as safe for any particular person.
- It has not been reviewed by a clinician.
- It cannot prevent a reader from ignoring it.

*This plan's purpose is to make the book's limits explicit to the reader, and to make them auditable by us.*
