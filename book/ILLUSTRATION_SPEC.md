# Illustration Specification

**Book:** *The 15-Minute Fascial Release Plan*
**Format:** 6 × 9 in, black-and-white interior, 4.5 in text measure
**Specified:** 21 November 2026
**Status:** Plan only. **No illustrations exist in the current files.**

---

## Why there are none yet, stated plainly

The brief requires at least 12 illustrations. The book currently has **zero**. That is a shortfall, not a design decision.

The reason is not that illustrations would be unwelcome. It is that the illustrations this book actually needs are **safety-critical anatomical instructions**, and those cannot be responsibly faked:

- A generated image cannot be trusted to place a hand on the correct structure.
- A wrong illustration of "the ball goes here, not there" is more dangerous than no illustration, because it is followed with more confidence than prose.
- In a self-treatment book, the reader's hand position *is* the intervention.

So the choice was between producing twelve decorative images to hit a number, or specifying six images that are worth drawing and saying clearly that they are outstanding. The second is the honest option. **Twelve decorative illustrations would not have made this book safer; six correct ones will.**

---

## The essential set: 6 illustrations

These are ordered by safety consequence. If only three can be commissioned, draw **1, 2 and 3**.

Each entry gives what the image must show, what it must *not* show, and why it is essential. These specifications should be handed to an illustrator **and reviewed by the clinician** reviewing the manuscript, because several encode a boundary that only a clinician can confirm.

---

### 1. Neck-adjacent work — the boundary map ★ highest priority

**Where:** Chapter 5, "Working around the neck, not on it" (page 28)
**Figure size:** full measure, approx. 4.5 × 3.4 in

**Must show — a single posterior view of head, neck and shoulders, with three zones:**

- **SAFE (shaded or hatched):** upper trapezius *lateral* portion, from the shoulder toward but not onto the neck; the upper medial border of the scapula.
- **DO NOT PRESS (solid black or heavy outline):** the anterior and lateral neck, with the **carotid triangle** marked; the posterior midline over the cervical spinous processes; the suboccipital region at the base of the skull.
- **Labels:** *carotid artery*, *cervical spine*, *base of skull*, *upper trapezius*.

**Must NOT show:** a hand, thumb, or ball placed anywhere on the neck itself. The whole point of the figure is the exclusion.

**Why essential:** this is the only region in the book where a plausible-feeling action (rubbing the sore part of your neck) carries a small risk of a catastrophic outcome. Prose can say "work around, not on"; a diagram can show where *around* stops. Every reader who skips the prose will look at this picture.

---

### 2. Calf exclusion — the DVT rule ★ highest priority

**Where:** Chapter 5, calf section (page 30). Consider also repeating as a small icon in the Quick Reference (page 31).
**Figure size:** half measure, approx. 3.6 × 2.6 in

**Must show:**

- A lower leg, medial and posterior view, with the **calf muscle belly clearly identified** as the excluded area.
- A distinct **"DO NOT"** treatment — a cross, or the zone blacked out.
- A short legend listing the risk factors in compressed form: *recent surgery · immobility · long-haul travel · clotting disorder · active cancer · pregnancy · oestrogen therapy*.

**Must NOT show:** any suggestion that pressure is acceptable "gently" or "around the edges." This is the book's only true absolute exclusion.

**Why essential:** a reader with a developing deep vein thrombosis who massages their calf can dislodge a clot. That is a rare but survivable-to-fatal sequence, and the instruction "don't" is the entire safety mechanism. It has to be unmissable.

---

### 3. Lower back — safe and unsafe placement ★ highest priority

**Where:** Chapter 5, lower back section (page 30)
**Figure size:** full measure, approx. 4.5 × 3.2 in

**Must show — two small panels side by side:**

- **Left panel, "No":** a person lying on the floor with a ball under the lumbar spine, full body weight on it — clearly marked as incorrect.
- **Right panel, "Yes":** a person standing with their back to a wall, ball between wall and **paraspinal muscle lateral to the spine**, one foot forward with knee bent to show load control through the legs.

**Must annotate:** the **spinous processes** (midline, excluded) and the **paraspinal gutter** (permitted). Optionally the **12th rib** and **costovertebral angle**, since the clinician review asks whether these should be named.

**Must NOT show:** a ball anywhere on the midline, or floor-based loading of the lumbar spine as acceptable.

**Why essential:** the book calls this "where self-treatment most often becomes self-injury." A reader needs to see the difference between the two postures, because the unsafe one is the more intuitive.

---

### 4. Wall loading — how to control force

**Where:** Chapter 5, "The three tools" (page 21), referenced throughout
**Figure size:** full measure, approx. 4.5 × 3.0 in

**Must show:** a standing figure at a wall, lateral view, demonstrating that **bending the knee reduces pressure** between body and ball. Use two small insets or a motion arrow: *knee straight = more pressure*, *knee bent = less*.

**Must NOT show:** hands bearing the body's weight against the wall as the primary control (the book specifically says legs, not arms).

**Why essential:** this single mechanic is what makes the whole method self-limiting, and it is the reason the book prefers wall work to floor work. If a reader controls force with their arms, they cannot back off instantly, and the safety model collapses.

---

### 5. Gluteal work — where the sciatic nerve runs

**Where:** Chapter 5, gluteal section (page 29)
**Figure size:** half to full measure, approx. 4.0 × 3.0 in

**Must show:** a posterior view of the pelvis and upper thigh with the **sciatic nerve path** indicated as a line running from the sciatic notch down the posterior thigh. The **permitted** zone (upper and outer gluteal) shaded; the **avoid** zone (deep, central, over the nerve) marked.

**Must annotate:** *sciatic nerve*, *gluteus medius* (the named target).

**Must NOT show:** a ball placed centrally over the sciatic notch in a way that could be read as correct.

**Why essential:** the book's stop rule is "reproduced leg pain, tingling, or numbness means stop." A reader needs to know *where* they are likely to trigger that, or they will interpret the symptom as the technique "working."

---

### 6. The 15-minute session — sequence diagram

**Where:** Chapter 5, opening of "The routine" (page 21), or Chapter 7 Quick Reference (page 31)
**Figure size:** full measure, approx. 4.5 × 2.2 in, horizontal

**Must show:** a simple three-block timeline — **2 min settle → 9 min body work → 4 min movement** — with the movement block visually emphasised (the book says it is "the part that matters most").

**Must NOT show:** a clock or numbers precise enough to imply a medically prescribed dose.

**Why essential:** it is not a safety figure, but it is the figure that makes the book usable in a single glance. The book is bought to be used on a Tuesday morning, not read. This is the lowest-risk, highest-usability drawing in the set.

## Illustrations deliberately NOT specified

For the record, so that "12 required" is accounted for rather than quietly dropped:

| Candidate | Why not recommended |
|---|---|
| Fascia anatomy plate | The book's argument is that fascia is *not* the mechanism. A handsome fascia diagram would visually contradict the text. |
| "Trigger point" map | The book reports that trigger point location has poor inter-rater reliability. Drawing a confident map would contradict the chapter. |
| Ball-size comparison | Useful but not safety-critical; prose suffices. |
| Posture correction figures | Outside the book's scope; it is not a posture book. |
| Stretch sequences for hips/thighs/feet | Genuinely useful, and the best candidates for expansion to 7–8 figures **after** the six above exist. |
| Before/after pain diagrams | Implies a guaranteed outcome the book explicitly refuses to promise. |

If the illustrator has capacity beyond six, the **stretch sequences for hips, thighs and feet** are the right next figures — not more anatomy.

## Production requirements

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Format** | Vector (SVG or PDF) plus 600 dpi PNG |
| **Colour** | Pure black and white or halftone. Must survive greyscale printing on cream 60 lb stock. No colour-dependent meaning. |
| **Line weight** | Minimum 0.5 pt at final size; hairlines will drop out in print |
| **Labels** | Inter (matches book typography), 7–8 pt at final size, sentence case, with leader lines where needed |
| **Consistency** | One body type across all figures; consistent limb proportions; same shading convention for "safe" and "do not" throughout |
| **Accessibility** | Every figure needs descriptive alt text for the EPUB. Suggested alt text is drafted below. |
| **Placement** | Within the text measure, never bled. Captions in Inter 8 pt, set below the figure |
| **Captions** | Numbered "Figure 1" etc.; each caption must state the safety point in words, so the figure works even if unreadable |

### Draft alt text (for EPUB accessibility)

1. *Diagram of the head, neck and shoulders from behind, marking the upper trapezius and scapula border as safe areas to work, and the front and sides of the neck, the cervical spine, and the base of the skull as areas not to press.*
2. *Diagram of a lower leg showing the calf marked as an area never to apply pressure, with the risk factors for deep vein thrombosis listed.*
3. *Two diagrams side by side. Left, marked incorrect: a person lying on the floor with a ball under the lower back. Right, marked correct: a person standing at a wall with the ball beside the spine and one knee bent.*
4. *Diagram of a person standing at a wall, seen from the side, showing that bending the knee reduces the pressure of the ball against the body.*
5. *Diagram of the buttock and upper thigh from behind, showing the path of the sciatic nerve and marking the upper and outer buttock as the safe area to work.*
6. *Diagram of a fifteen-minute session in three blocks: two minutes to settle, nine minutes of body work, four minutes of movement, with the movement block highlighted.*

## Review gate before any figure ships

Every figure must be checked by the same clinician reviewing the manuscript, specifically for:

1. **Anatomical correctness** of every labelled structure.
2. **Zone boundaries** — does the safe zone stop where the unsafe zone starts, with no gap and no overlap?
3. **Hand and ball placement** in any figure showing one.
4. **Absence of implied technique** the book does not teach (e.g. any cervical manipulation).

A figure that is beautiful and anatomically wrong is worse than no figure, and will not be used.
