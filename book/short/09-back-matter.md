## Glossary

**Cauda equina syndrome.** Compression of the bundle of nerve roots below the spinal cord, producing saddle numbness and bladder or bowel dysfunction. A surgical emergency.

**Certainty (of evidence).** A graded judgement, in systematic reviews, of how confident we can be that an estimated effect is close to the true one. "Moderate" is a specific rating, not a synonym for "proven."

**Contraindication.** A condition in which a technique should not be used, either absolutely or with modification.

**Densification.** A proposed change in which fascial layers become more adhesive and glide less freely. Supported by rheological, imaging, and therapeutic evidence; not confirmed histologically in humans.

**Fascia.** Connective tissue forming sheets and sheaths beneath the skin, enclosing and separating muscle and organs. See Chapter 2 for the two competing formal definitions.

**Fascial system.** The broader of the two definitions: the three-dimensional continuum of collagen-containing connective tissue throughout the body.

**Modic changes.** Changes in the vertebral bone adjacent to a disc, visible on MRI, of uncertain clinical significance.

**Myofascial release.** A family of manual techniques, practitioner-applied or self-applied, involving sustained pressure on soft tissue. Evidence for short-term benefit on pain is moderate in chronic low back pain and limited by lack of blinding in neck pain.

**Nociception.** The nervous system's detection and encoding of potentially damaging stimuli. Distinct from pain, which is the experience.

**Nociplastic pain.** Pain arising from altered nociception without clear evidence of tissue damage or nerve lesion. A mechanism descriptor, not a diagnosis.

**Red flag.** A symptom or history feature associated with a serious underlying condition, which warrants assessment rather than self-treatment.

**Spondylolysis.** A defect in the bony arch of a vertebra.

## About This Book

This book was researched and drafted with substantial assistance from an AI system working under a human-directed editorial process, and it is published transparently on that basis. It is not presented as the work of a clinician, and no clinical review has been undertaken.

Every effort has been made to represent the evidence accurately, including where that evidence is unflattering to the book's own subject. The sources are listed so that any claim can be checked. Where the underlying research was accessible only in abstract or summary form, the reference list says so rather than implying otherwise.

If you find an error, it is mine and not the sources'.
