# References

Every source below was consulted while writing this book. Where a work was consulted only at abstract or summary level, the entry does not imply a full-text reading.

## Pain, definition, and classification

**International Association for the Study of Pain.** Terminology: pain terms and definitions. iasp-pain.org/resources/terminology. *Consulted directly.* The definition of pain and the accompanying notes are IASP copyright; this book paraphrases them and quotes no wording verbatim, apart from a short phrase attributed in context.

**Raja SN, Carr DB, Cohen M, et al.** The revised International Association for the Study of Pain definition of pain: concepts, challenges, and compromises. *Pain.* 2020;161(9):1976–1982. doi:10.1097/j.pain.0000000000001939. *Consulted at full-text level via published PDF.*

## Fascia: anatomy, nomenclature, innervation

**Schleip R, Hedley G, Yucesoy CA.** Fascial nomenclature: update on related consensus process. *Clinical Anatomy.* 2019. doi:10.1002/ca.23423. *Consulted via published consensus summary.*

**Neumann DA.** Fascia, eh. What is it? What is it good for? *Clinical Anatomy.* 2026. doi:10.1002/ca.70047. *Consulted via published summary and comparison table.*

**Suarez-Rodriguez V, et al.** Evidence of a new hidden neural network into deep fasciae. *Scientific Reports.* 2021. doi:10.1038/s41598-021-92194-z. *Consulted via published results.*

**The muscular fascia as a mechanobiological and stem-cell compartment: an umbrella review of its biology, pathology, diagnostics and therapeutics.** *Journal of Clinical Physiology, Pathology and Medical Research.* 2026. *Consulted via published results and discussion.* Source of the statements on site-specific proprioception, the absence of corpuscular receptors in thoracolumbar fascia and fascia lata, the absence of human histological confirmation for densification, and the critical appraisal of systematic-review confidence.

**Sensory innervation of muscular fascia: a review.** Cited in this book only for the finding that most fascial innervation studies were conducted in the twentieth century and were of poor methodological quality, and that Ruffini corpuscles have been clearly identified in a small number of studies. *Consulted via secondary reporting.*

## Myofascial release and manual therapy evidence

**Systematic review and meta-analysis of myofascial release for chronic low back pain.** *Frontiers in Medicine.* 2021. doi:10.3389/fmed.2021.697986. *Consulted via published GRADE table.* Eight RCTs, 375 participants; moderate certainty for pain and function; no significant effect on quality of life, mental health, pressure pain threshold, or trunk mobility.

**Effectiveness of myofascial release for adults with chronic neck pain: a meta-analysis.** *Physiotherapy.* 2023. *Consulted via published results.* Ten RCTs, 549 participants; significant pain reduction; methodological quality rated good to excellent with absence of blinding in all but one trial.

**Myofascial release as a treatment for orthopaedic conditions: a systematic review.** *Journal of Manual and Manipulative Therapy.* PMID 23725488. *Consulted via abstract.* Ten studies, PEDro 6–8, few conclusions possible because of low study quality.

## A note on what is not cited

Several claims common in writing about fascia are absent from this book because no adequate source was located. These include the ideas that fascia stores emotion or trauma, that pressure releases toxins, that fascial restriction can be reliably diagnosed by touch, and that myofascial release restructures collagen. Where the book discusses these, it does so in order to say that the evidence does not support them.
